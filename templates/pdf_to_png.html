How to convert a PDF to PNG

  • 1 Drop your PDF on the box above, or click it and choose a file. The conversion starts as soon as the file is selected.
  • 2 Wait a few seconds. Each page is drawn as its own PNG image, up to 20 pages.
  • 3 On the results page, press "Download All Images (ZIP)", or use the "Download 1", "Download 2" links to save single pages.
  • 4 Open the ZIP: the images are named page_1.png, page_2.png and so on, in page order. Single-page downloads keep a long ID in front of the name, so rename them if you need to.

What quality do the PNG files have?

PNG is a lossless format, so saving a page as PNG throws nothing away: the file holds exactly the pixels the converter drew. The limit on quality is the size those pixels are drawn at. Every page is rendered at 2× the PDF's own size, which is 144 pixels per inch: an A4 page becomes 1190 × 1684 px and a US Letter page 1224 × 1584 px. There is no setting to choose a different resolution.

That is sharp when you view the image at 100% on a screen, and fine for slides, web pages and documentation. It is not a 300 DPI print export, and because the page's text and lines have been turned into pixels, zooming in far enough shows them. Pages larger than about 30 megapixels at 2× (poster-sized pages) are scaled down just enough to fit; ordinary pages never are.

Can I get a transparent background?

No. PNG can hold transparency, but this tool never produces it: every image it makes has a white or page-colored background and no transparency channel, and anything see-through in the PDF is flattened onto white. The measured test is in the results below. Remove Background is a separate tool for cutting a subject out of a photo; we have not tested it on document pages, so we can't promise a clean result there.

PNG or JPG?

Pick PNG for text, line art and diagrams, and PDF to JPG for photographic or scanned pages, where JPG files are several times smaller. Our guide has the measured comparison.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the PDF to PNG converter free?

Yes. It is free, needs no account, and adds no watermark. Uploaded PDFs and the PNG images made from them are deleted automatically after about five minutes.

What resolution are the PNG files?

Every page is rendered at 2× the PDF's own size, which is 144 pixels per inch: an A4 page comes out at 1190 × 1684 pixels. You can't choose a different resolution. Pages that would be larger than about 30 megapixels, such as posters, are scaled down just enough to fit.

Can I convert a PDF to PNG without losing quality?

The PNG format itself loses nothing, so the conversion adds no compression damage. What you get is the page drawn as pixels at 144 DPI, which is a fixed size: text and lines are no longer sharp vector shapes, and the image can't be enlarged cleanly.

Will the PNGs have a transparent background?

No. Every PNG is RGB with no transparency channel. A page with no background of its own comes out white, and anything see-through in the PDF is flattened onto white. The tool has no option to keep transparency.

Why is my PNG file larger than I expected?

PNG keeps every pixel, so the file size depends on what is on the page. Text and line art stay small, but pages with photos or scanned images produce files many times larger, as the measured table above shows. If size matters more than exact pixels, PDF to JPG makes the same pages much smaller.

Is there a page limit?

Yes. Only the first 20 pages are converted, and the results page just says "20 page(s) converted" without noting that pages were left out, so count the images you get back. Our guide shows how to convert the rest of a longer PDF.

Can I download just one page instead of the whole ZIP?

Yes. The results page gives you a "download all" ZIP button as well as an individual download link for every page.

Can I convert a password-protected PDF?

No. The tool can't open a PDF that needs a password, and the conversion fails with an error. Remove the password first with Unlock PDF, then convert the unlocked copy.
