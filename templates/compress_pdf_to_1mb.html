Compress PDF to 1MB

Shrink your PDF to fit a 1MB upload limit — the size cap used by most job applications, university portals, and government forms.

Why 1MB specifically?

1MB has become an informal standard cap for online forms: job application portals, university admissions systems, visa and government document uploaders, and many HR systems reject anything larger. It's small enough for their servers to handle in bulk, but usually still large enough for a multi-page scanned document or a text-based PDF with a few images.

How this tool tries to hit 1MB

Rather than applying one fixed setting, this tool compresses your file, checks the resulting size, and — if it's still over 1MB — automatically re-compresses with a stronger setting and tries again, escalating through increasingly aggressive image downsampling until the file fits or no further reduction is possible.

This is a best-effort process, not a guarantee. A PDF that's mostly sharp, high-resolution photos can usually be squeezed under 1MB. A PDF that's already 1MB of pure text (rare, since text compresses extremely well) may not shrink much further, because there's no image data left to downsample.

If your file still won't fit under 1MB

  • Split it first. Use Split PDF to break a long document into smaller sections, then compress each one — many upload forms accept multiple smaller files.
  • Remove pages you don't need. A cover sheet, blank page, or duplicate scan adds size for no benefit — try Delete Pages first.
  • Re-scan at a lower resolution. If the original is a phone scan, a 300 DPI scan compresses to 1MB far more easily than a 600+ DPI one.
  • Use the full Compress PDF tool and manually pick "Extreme" — it converts images to grayscale, which shrinks colour scans significantly more than the automatic target-size loop alone if colour isn't required.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my PDF always end up exactly at or under 1MB?

Not always — see above. The tool always returns the smallest version it could produce, even if that's slightly over 1MB, rather than degrading the document until it's unreadable.

Does hitting 1MB mean my text becomes blurry?

No. Text is vector data and is never downsampled or blurred by compression — only embedded images are affected. A text-only PDF will look identical after compression.

What's the difference between this and the regular Compress PDF tool?

The Compress PDF tool lets you pick a fixed quality level (Low/Medium/High/Extreme) or type in any target size you want. This page is a shortcut that's pre-set to target exactly 1MB, since that's the size most people searching for this tool actually need.

Is this different from compressing for email?

Yes — Compress PDF for Email applies one fixed high-compression setting tuned for typical email attachment limits (20–25MB), while this page keeps re-compressing until the file is at or near exactly 1MB, whatever that takes.
