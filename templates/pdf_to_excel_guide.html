How to Convert PDF to Excel

Many reports, invoices, financial statements, and data exports are distributed as PDF files. While PDFs are great for viewing, they're not designed for data analysis. Converting a PDF to Excel extracts the tabular data into a spreadsheet where you can sort, filter, calculate, and analyse it freely.

Our free online PDF to Excel converter extracts tables from your PDF and saves them as a .xlsx file that opens directly in Microsoft Excel, Google Sheets, or any compatible spreadsheet application.

Step 1 – Open the PDF to Excel Tool

Go to our PDF to Excel converter. You'll see a file upload area where you can click to select your PDF or drag and drop it onto the page.

Step 2 – Upload Your PDF

Select your PDF and upload it. The tool analyses the document, detects tables on each page, and extracts the data from them. Each table found is converted into rows and columns in the Excel spreadsheet.

For best results, the PDF should contain clear, well-structured tables with visible borders or defined column/row layouts. Unstructured text or complex multi-column layouts may not convert cleanly.

Step 3 – Download the Excel File

Once the conversion is complete, the .xlsx file downloads automatically. Open it in Excel or Google Sheets to view and work with your data. Review the data to check that all columns and rows were detected correctly.

Tips for Better PDF to Excel Conversion

  • Clean, structured tables convert best: PDFs with clearly defined tables (borders, aligned columns) produce the most accurate Excel output. Irregular or merged cells may require manual cleanup after conversion.
  • Text-based PDFs only: The tool extracts data from text-based PDFs. Scanned PDFs are images and their tables cannot be automatically detected and extracted.
  • Check for merged cells: Complex table headers that span multiple columns may not convert exactly. Review the Excel file after conversion and adjust any merged cells as needed.
  • Multi-page tables: If a table spans multiple pages of the PDF, each page's portion will be extracted separately and combined into the spreadsheet.
  • No table found: If the PDF has data presented as plain text rather than a table, the tool may not extract it. In this case, use our PDF to Text tool to extract the raw text, then format it manually in Excel.

Common Uses for PDF to Excel Conversion

Financial reports and statements: Bank statements, annual reports, and financial summaries are often shared as PDFs. Converting them to Excel lets you perform calculations and analysis directly.

Government and regulatory data: Data released by government agencies is often in PDF tables. Converting to Excel makes it easy to work with for research or compliance purposes.

Invoice processing: Businesses that receive many invoices in PDF format can extract the data into Excel for accounting, budgeting, or reconciliation.

Survey and research results: Research papers often include data tables in PDF format. Converting them to Excel enables further analysis and chart creation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why doesn't the tool find a table in my PDF?

The tool detects tables by looking for structured row-and-column layouts. If the data is presented as free-flowing text or in a complex layout without clear table structure, it may not be detected. Try our PDF to Text tool to extract the raw content instead.

Can I convert a scanned PDF to Excel?

Scanned PDFs are images and cannot be automatically parsed for table data. You would need OCR (optical character recognition) software to first extract the text, then structure it as a table.

Will all tables from multi-page PDFs be included?

Yes. The tool scans every page and extracts any tables it finds across the entire document.

What should I do if the data looks wrong after conversion?

Minor errors in column alignment are common with complex tables. Open the Excel file, compare it to the original PDF, and manually correct any misaligned cells or missing data.
