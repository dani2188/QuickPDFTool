Real Results: What to Expect

We converted four sample PDFs with this tool. Every page becomes one PNG at 2× the PDF's own size: a PDF page is measured in points (72 to an inch), so 2× is 144 pixels per inch. The PNG files carry no resolution (DPI) tag, so go by the pixel size; if a program asks for a DPI value you will have to set it yourself.

  • No transparency: every PNG we checked is 8-bit RGB with no transparency (alpha) channel. We tested a page with no background and a 40%-opaque red rectangle: the PNG is RGB, the corner is white (255, 255, 255) and the rectangle came out as solid pale red (255, 153, 153), not see-through. Anything see-through in the PDF is flattened onto white.
  • Background color: in the corner of every image from the text report, line-art diagram, photo brochure samples the color was pure white (255, 255, 255), because those PDFs have no background of their own. The scanned letter keeps the paper tone that is part of its scanned image (RGB 241, 241, 241), and the tool does not whiten it.
  • Page limit: a 25-page test PDF produced 20 PNGs, so count the images you get back, because the results page only says "20 page(s) converted" and does not mention that pages were left out.
  • Very large pages: an A0 poster page (2384 × 3370 pt) came out at 4607 × 6513 px, a little under 2× because very large pages are scaled to a limit of about 30 megapixels. Normal pages are never scaled down.
