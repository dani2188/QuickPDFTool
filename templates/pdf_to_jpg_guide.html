How to Convert PDF to JPG

Converting a PDF to JPG turns each page of your document into a separate image file. This is useful when you need to use PDF content in a presentation, post it online, embed pages in a website, or share specific pages as pictures rather than documents.

Unlike sending a PDF, images can be viewed and embedded anywhere — in emails, social media, design tools, word processors, and more. Our free tool converts each PDF page to a high-quality JPG image with no software installation required.

Step 1 – Open the PDF to JPG Tool

Go to our PDF to JPG converter. You'll see a file upload area. Click to browse for your PDF or drag and drop it directly onto the page.

Step 2 – Upload Your PDF

Select your PDF file and upload it. The tool supports any standard PDF, including multi-page documents. It will process all pages and convert each one into a separate JPG image.

The tool renders each page at 2x resolution (200% zoom) to ensure the resulting images are sharp and detailed, sharp for screen viewing. For print, see the notes further down.

Step 3 – Download the JPG Images

Once conversion is complete, you'll see a gallery of all the extracted page images. You can download any individual image, or download all images at once as a ZIP archive. Each image file is named by its page number for easy identification.

Tips for Converting PDF to JPG

  • Multi-page PDFs: The tool processes up to 20 pages. For longer documents, consider splitting the PDF first using the Split PDF tool, then converting each part.
  • Use for presentations: PDF slides can be converted to JPG and imported directly into PowerPoint, Google Slides, or Keynote as image slides.
  • Website thumbnails: Convert the first page of your PDF to a JPG to create a preview thumbnail for your website or download page.
  • Email inline images: Unlike PDFs, JPG images can be embedded directly in email bodies so recipients see the content immediately without opening an attachment.
  • For editing: If you need to annotate or edit a PDF page using an image editor, converting it to JPG first is the easiest approach.

Common Use Cases

Social media posts: Share individual pages or infographics from a PDF as images on Instagram, LinkedIn, or Twitter without requiring followers to download a file.

Embedding in documents: Insert specific PDF pages as images into a Word document, Google Doc, or email newsletter.

Creating a preview: Show a thumbnail preview of a PDF on your website so visitors can see what a document looks like before downloading it.

Design and editing: Designers often need individual pages as images to use them as backgrounds or references in graphic design software like Photoshop or Canva.

Archiving form data: Convert a filled-out PDF form to images to create a visual archive that anyone can view without needing a PDF reader.

When the Result Isn't What You Expected

The text in the images can't be selected, searched or clicked

Each page becomes a flat picture. Text, links and form fields are no longer text, links or fields, and the images carry none of the PDF's search or copy ability. If you need the words, run the original PDF through PDF to Text or PDF to Word instead of reading them back out of the JPGs.

Page 30 of a 45-page PDF never shows up

Only the first 20 pages are converted, so a page later in the file is not in the results however you download them. When you want one late page, cut it out first with Split PDF, or remove the earlier pages with Delete PDF Pages, then convert the smaller file.

Small text looks soft when the image is printed or zoomed in

The images are made at twice the PDF's own page size, which is generous for a screen and thin for a page printed at full size. We have not tested printed output, so check one printout before you commit a batch. Dense small print, such as a footnote or a table of figures, shows the softness first.

A faint haze or fringe around text and thin lines

JPG is built for photographs, and sharp black-on-white edges are where it struggles. A page that is mostly text, a diagram or a spreadsheet can show a slight halo around the letters. For pages like that, PDF to PNG keeps edges clean at the cost of bigger files.

You wanted the photos inside the PDF, not pictures of the pages

This tool photographs whole pages, so a photo in the PDF comes out with the page text and margins around it. To get the embedded pictures themselves, use Extract Images.

"Couldn't process that" or "doesn't look like a valid PDF"

The first appears for a password-protected PDF, which has to be opened with Unlock PDF before it can be converted. The second means the file is damaged or is not a PDF at all, for example a download that stopped partway. Opening it in a PDF viewer will show which case you have.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I convert PDF to PNG instead?

Yes. Use our PDF to PNG converter if you need transparent backgrounds or lossless image quality.

Will the text in my PDF be readable in the JPG images?

Yes. The conversion renders the page at high resolution, so text remains sharp and legible in the resulting images, especially at 2x zoom.

Does this work for scanned PDFs?

Yes. Scanned PDFs are already image-based, so they convert particularly well. The result will look exactly like the scanned original.

Why do my images come out in different sizes?

Each page is rendered at twice its own size, so a PDF that mixes portrait pages, landscape pages or different paper sizes produces images of different dimensions. Nothing is wrong; the images follow the pages.

Will pages with a coloured or transparent background keep it?

Pages are drawn onto a plain image with no transparency, and JPG cannot hold transparency in any case. Anything that was see-through in the PDF is shown as it looks on screen against white.

Can I turn the JPG images back into text?

Not with this tool. It does not read text from images. Convert the original PDF with PDF to Text instead, which works from the PDF's own text where it has any.
