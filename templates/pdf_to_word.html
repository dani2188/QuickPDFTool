Why Convert a PDF to Word?

Edit locked content: PDFs are designed to be read, not edited. Converting to Word lets you update text, change formatting, and modify the document freely.

Reuse content: Copy text, tables, and headings from a converted Word file without the limitations of copying from a PDF viewer.

Collaborate in Word: Share a DOCX file with colleagues so they can use tracked changes, comments, and revision history — features not available in PDF.

Update outdated documents: When you only have the PDF version of a document that needs updating, converting to Word is the fastest way to get an editable copy.

Tips for Better Conversion Results

Conversion works best on text-based PDFs (documents originally created digitally in Word or similar software). Scanned PDFs are images of pages rather than real text, so the conversion may produce limited results. If you have a scanned document, extracting the text first can help.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will the formatting be preserved?

For digitally created PDFs, most formatting including headings, paragraphs, and basic layout is preserved. Complex elements like multi-column layouts, tables, or special fonts may require minor adjustments after conversion.

Does this work with scanned PDFs?

Scanned PDFs are images, not text, so conversion accuracy depends on the scan quality. For best results with scanned documents, try the PDF to Text tool first.

What software do I need to open the DOCX file?

Any modern word processor can open DOCX files, including Microsoft Word (Windows and Mac), Google Docs (free, browser-based), and LibreOffice Writer (free desktop app).

Is there a page limit?

There is no page limit, but the maximum file size is 10 MB. Compress your PDF first if it exceeds this limit.

Can I convert Word back to PDF?

Yes. After editing in Word, use our Word to PDF tool to convert it back to a PDF document.
