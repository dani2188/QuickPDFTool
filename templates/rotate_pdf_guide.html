How to Rotate PDF Pages

Scanned documents often come out sideways or upside down. Pages saved from mobile apps or emails can have the wrong orientation. Rotating a PDF fixes these issues and makes the document easy to read and share.

Our free online PDF rotation tool lets you rotate all pages in a PDF by 90, 180, or 270 degrees in just a few clicks. No installation, no account required.

Step 1 – Open the Rotate PDF Tool

Navigate to our Rotate PDF tool. You'll see a file upload area where you can click to select your PDF or drag and drop it from your file manager.

Step 2 – Choose Your Rotation Angle

After uploading your file, select the rotation angle you need:

  • 90° clockwise: Rotates all pages a quarter turn to the right. Use this if pages are rotated 90° to the left (text reads up the side).
  • 180°: Flips all pages upside down. Use this for completely inverted documents.
  • 270° clockwise (90° counter-clockwise): Rotates pages a quarter turn to the left.

Think about which direction your pages need to go. If text is running from bottom to top along the right side, you need a 90° clockwise rotation. If it's running from top to bottom on the left, you need 270°.

Step 3 – Download the Rotated PDF

After setting the rotation, the tool processes your document and produces a new PDF with all pages correctly oriented. Download the file to your device — it will open in the correct orientation in any PDF viewer.

Tips for Rotating PDFs

  • Check all pages first: If only some pages are rotated incorrectly, consider splitting the PDF first, rotating just those pages, then merging everything back together.
  • Scanned documents: Rotation is especially common with scanned documents. After rotating, you may want to compress the file to reduce its size.
  • Before merging: If you are about to merge multiple PDFs, make sure all their pages are correctly oriented first. Fix rotations before merging.
  • Landscape vs portrait: Some documents are intentionally landscape (wide). Make sure you're rotating a document that actually needs it, not one that's meant to be horizontal.

Common Reasons to Rotate a PDF

Scanned pages are sideways: Placing a document sideways in a scanner is a very common mistake. A quick 90° rotation fixes it instantly.

Mobile phone scans: Photos taken of documents on a phone often inherit the phone's orientation at capture time. Rotation corrects these.

Received documents: Sometimes files are emailed or downloaded with incorrect orientation from the sender's side. You can fix them locally without going back to the sender.

Preparing for printing: Printing a sideways document wastes paper and is unreadable. Always check orientation before printing and rotate if needed.

When the Rotation Doesn't Fix It

Most pages are sideways but a few are fine

Rotation applies to every page, so the order of work matters. If most of the pages are wrong, rotate the whole file first, and then deal with the few pages that are now wrong. If most pages are right and only a few are wrong, pull those few out with Split PDF, rotate them separately, and rejoin everything with Merge PDF. Whichever way you go, look through the finished file page by page, since the pieces are put back in the order you give them.

You rotated the wrong way

Rotation adds up on each pass. If a 90° turn went the wrong direction, you do not have to go back to the original: turn the result by 180° to undo it and go the right way, or run the original again with the other angle. If the upload page confuses you, a good rule is to picture the top of the text. Where it points left, the page needs a clockwise turn; where it points right, it needs the other one.

The scan is only a little crooked

The tool turns pages in steps of 90°, 180° and 270°. Any other value is refused with a message asking for one of those three. A scan that is a few degrees off needs deskewing in a scanning or image application. This tool cannot straighten it.

Bookmarks and the document title disappeared

The result is written as a new file. In our test, a three-page PDF with a table of contents and a title and author came back with no bookmarks and without the title and author, while its clickable web links were still working on each page. If your file relies on a bookmarked outline, plan to recreate it after rotating.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will rotation affect the text content of my PDF?

No. Rotation is a display operation — the text, images, and all content within the PDF remain unchanged. Only the viewing orientation is modified.

Does the rotated file look the same in all PDF viewers?

Yes. The rotation is saved into the PDF file itself, so it displays correctly in Adobe Acrobat, Chrome, Edge, Preview on Mac, and any other standard PDF viewer.

Can I rotate a password-protected PDF?

You'll need to remove the password first, then rotate the pages, and optionally re-protect the file afterwards.

Will rotating change the size of the file much?

Very little. Our measurements on the tool page show a difference of a few hundred bytes for small test files. We have not measured large scanned documents.

Will page numbers and headers rotate too?

Yes. They are part of the page, so they turn with it. The text stays unchanged and only the page's display direction is altered.
