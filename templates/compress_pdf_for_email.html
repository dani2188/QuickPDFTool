Why compress PDF for email?

Many email services limit attachment sizes. Large PDF documents may not be accepted or may take a long time to upload. Compressing your PDF reduces file size while keeping the document readable.

The hidden reason attachments bounce below the stated limit

Email attachments aren't sent as raw bytes — they're base64-encoded first, which inflates the file by roughly 37% before it ever reaches your provider's limit check. A file that's 24MB on your disk can become over 32MB in transit, which is why an attachment can bounce even though it looks well under the stated cap. As a rule of thumb, aim for your compressed PDF to be no more than 70–75% of the provider's stated limit to leave room for that overhead.

Email attachment size limits (and the safe target to aim for)

  • Gmail: 25 MB limit — aim for under ~18 MB
  • Outlook / Microsoft 365: 20 MB limit — aim for under ~14 MB
  • Yahoo Mail: 25 MB limit — aim for under ~18 MB
  • Company/corporate mail servers: often much stricter (5–10 MB) — check with your IT department if unsure

This tool applies a high-compression preset tuned specifically for this situation, downsampling images to a web-friendly resolution so text-heavy reports and scanned documents both land comfortably under typical limits in one pass.

If it's still too large

Very large or image-dense PDFs (50+ pages, high-resolution scans) may not compress enough to email directly. In that case, either split the file into smaller sections and send them separately, or share it as a link via Google Drive, Dropbox, or OneDrive instead of attaching it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why did my attachment bounce even though it's under the size limit?

Because of base64 encoding overhead (see above) — the file grows by roughly a third once it's actually transmitted as an attachment. Compress it further, or send it as a cloud storage link instead.

Will this reduce quality more than the regular Compress PDF tool?

It uses a similarly strong setting to the "High" level on the regular Compress PDF tool, downsampling images to 72 DPI — good for on-screen reading, not ideal if the recipient needs to print at full quality.

What if I need an exact file size instead?

Use Compress PDF to 1MB or the target size option on the main Compress PDF tool if you need to hit a specific number rather than "small enough for email."
